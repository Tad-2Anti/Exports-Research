## 1. Business identity

City Exports is based in India and operates as a merchant exporter. It sources from independent producers and suppliers, primarily in Tamil Nadu and nearby Kerala, Karnataka and Telangana, and sells to overseas buyers.

Public website: [cityexports.ltd](https://cityexports.ltd/)

## 2. Current objective

Build a repeatable system that helps the user research and learn buyer procurement while AI agents gather and screen relevant overseas accounts.

For each research cycle, the practical target is:

- approximately 25 raw candidates
- approximately 12–15 screened prospects
- 7–8 high-confidence target accounts
- active demand only when supported by a dated request, RFQ, buyer confirmation, sample request or response

The target number is not a reason to keep weak companies. Report fewer when evidence does not support more.

## 4. Evidence model

Keep these evidence classes separate.

### Import/market proof

Evidence that Norway imports or uses a product/category: official trade statistics, HS-code data, market reports and repeated import activity.

This does not prove that a named company wants a new supplier.

### Buyer/business fit

Evidence that a company handles the exact product and has a plausible external purchasing model: official product pages, own-label manufacturing, importing, distribution, packing, contract production or disclosed overseas sourcing.

This is a relevant prospect, not necessarily an active buyer.

### Response proof

Evidence from a real procurement conversation: named buyer response, written requirement, RFQ, quantity, specification, buying window, sample request, quotation request, trial or order.

This is the strongest evidence and must never be implied from public catalogue information.

### Evidence labels

- **FACT:** directly supported by a source.
- **INFERENCE:** a reasonable interpretation that still needs confirmation.
- **UNKNOWN:** not established.

## 5. Buying-intent lanes

### Lane A — active opportunity

A dated, product-specific sourcing request, RFQ, buyer confirmation or active procurement conversation exists. Verify that it is still open.

### Lane B — strong target

The exact product and purchasing model are supported, but current buying need is unknown or evergreen.

Most public research will produce Lane B. It must not be presented as an open order.

## 7. Product and procurement qualification

Before costing or quoting, establish:

- exact product, grade and intended use
- technical specification and acceptable variation
- regulatory, food-safety or cosmetic requirements
- packaging and pack size
- annual or trial quantity
- buying window and next review/reorder period
- destination and delivery terms
- sample and testing expectations
- documentation and certification requirements
- whether the buyer imports directly or uses a distributor
- current origin/supplier situation where the buyer is willing to disclose it
- supplier onboarding and audit requirements

For essential oils, ask about botanical identity, origin, GC-MS requirements, pack size, annual volume, next review/reorder month and landed-price comparison. Do not assume raw oil, premixed blend and finished goods are interchangeable.

## 8. Contact standard

Find the sourcing owner, category buyer, procurement manager, product-development owner or approved supplier route where possible.

If a named person is unavailable:

- record `sourcing owner not reached`
- provide the official contact form, switchboard or supplier portal
- distinguish procurement from sales, customer service and order desks

Never guess personal email addresses. Apollo-like enrichment tools may help locate people, but they do not prove buying intent.

## 9. Required company record

Every retained record should contain:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Account identity | Legal name, website, Norway location and organisation number where verified |
| Exact product | Product, grade/use and link to evidence |
| Buying model | Importer, distributor, own-label brand, processor, manufacturer, packer or unresolved |
| Evidence | Exact URLs, dates and concise factual findings |
| Current activity | Active, unclear or inactive, with evidence |
| Origin/supplier | Verified disclosure or `UNKNOWN` |
| Intent | Lane A, Lane B or reject/hold |
| Contact | Verified person or official route; no guessed details |
| Fit hypothesis | One specific supportable reason to approach |
| Unknowns | What still requires buyer confirmation |
| Next action | One practical next step |
| Tracking | New, reopened, existing, contacted, replied, sample/RFQ/order |

## 10. Final decision rule

The system is successful when it gives the user a short, evidence-backed list of companies worth approaching and makes the next conversation more informed.

It is not successful merely because it produces many names, emails, scraped product pages or polished lead scores.
